# vis_story1 - visualisation specifics for story1

## scope

per-step visualisation specifics for the eighteen steps in story1.md. each step lists the catalog representations from idea1.md that get unfolded at that step. for each representation, an entry specifies what is laid down statically, what is layered on after, what is animated, and how the same beat replays at scale.

## relationship to math/docs/vis_principles.md

all principles in vis_principles.md apply to every entry below. per-step entries do not restate them; if an entry appears to conflict with a principle, the principle wins.

## relationship to math/docs/idea1.md and math/docs/story1.md

idea1.md is the source of catalog representations. story1.md is the order in which they surface. this file is how each one looks on screen.

## step 3 - fully-connected network, composition of neurons into layers

- the layered-graph drawing representation:
  - laid down statically: empty canvas; no structure yet
  - layered on after: input-layer neurons appear one at a time as circles in a vertical stack on the left side of the canvas; hidden-layer neurons appear one at a time as a second vertical stack to the right of the input layer; output-layer neurons appear as a third vertical stack on the right side; edges draw one at a time between every neuron of one layer and every neuron of the next layer, until the "fully connected" property is visible in the topology with no edge missing
  - animated moves: each neuron arrives with a small grow-from-zero motion; each edge draws as a line growing from its source neuron to its target neuron at a steady pace, in left-to-right order; brief zoom into the gap between two neurons after the first few edges have been drawn so the meaning of "an edge connects two specific neurons" lands close-up, then zoom back out to continue the drawing
  - scaling pattern: as a second beat after the slate clears, the same beat replays on a larger network (5 input neurons, 10 hidden neurons in each of two hidden layers, 3 output neurons) at higher speed; neurons appear in layer-batches rather than one at a time, edges draw in waves; the layer-by-layer construction order is preserved so the eye recognises the same pattern at scale; the smallest case is 2 input neurons, 2 hidden neurons in a single hidden layer, 1 output neuron - the minimum that lets the "fully-connected" property be visible

## step 6 - network as a system of equations, every variable written out explicitly

- the unrolled scalar-equation form representation:
  - laid down statically: clean canvas; the smallest fully-connected network (2 input neurons, 2 hidden neurons, 1 output neuron) is redrawn rapidly in the upper region of the canvas with every label in place from the start (input variables x_1 and x_2 on the input neurons; hidden variables h_1 and h_2 on the hidden neurons; output variable y on the output neuron; weights w_1 and w_2 above the edges feeding h_1; weights w_3 and w_4 above the edges feeding h_2; weights w_5 and w_6 above the edges feeding y; biases b_1 and b_2 beside the hidden neurons; bias b_3 beside the output neuron); the rebuild is short because the labelled network has been seen in earlier steps; the lower region of the canvas is empty and waiting
  - layered on after: equation slots appear in the lower region only as they get filled by the animation - no empty equation slots are pre-drawn
  - animated moves: the output label of h_1 lifts off its neuron, traces a short curved path down into the equation region, and lands as the left side of the first equation slot, becoming "h_1 ="; the input-variable labels x_1 and x_2 each duplicate themselves, fly down, and slot into their positions in the right-hand side of the expression; the weight labels w_1 and w_2 duplicate, fly down, and slot into their multiplication positions; the bias label b_1 duplicates, flies down, and slots in at the end; the activation function name then wraps the inner expression; the completed equation reads "h_1 = activation(x_1 * w_1 + x_2 * w_2 + b_1)"; throughout this animation the originals stay in place on the network diagram with their labels intact, only duplicates fly into the equation; the same animation then replays for h_2 below the first equation, and for y below the second; three equations stacked in the equation region by the end; brief zoom into the just-completed h_1 equation to highlight the term-by-term mapping (each x times a w, plus the bias, wrapped by an activation), then zoom back out before the h_2 animation begins
  - scaling pattern: as a second beat after the slate clears, the same animation replays on the larger network (5 input neurons, 10 hidden neurons per hidden layer, 3 output neurons); the per-neuron flying-down animation runs at full speed for one representative neuron of each layer; the remaining equations for the same layer flash in one after another at higher speed to make the uniformity of the pattern visible; the final equation block on the larger network is a tall stack with every neuron's equation present in order, with the equations for each layer grouped vertically

- the forward-pass view:
  - laid down statically: clean canvas; the smallest fully-connected network with full labels is redrawn rapidly in the upper region (no equation region this time); numeric input values appear next to the input neurons (e.g. x_1 = 0.3, x_2 = 0.7)
  - layered on after: a thin value-channel appears alongside each edge, ready to carry a number from the edge's source neuron to its target neuron
  - animated moves: the numeric input values flow along each edge that the value participates in; the visual thickness or brightness of each flowing trail is scaled by the magnitude of the weight on that edge - small weight gives a thin or dim trail, large weight gives a thick or bright trail - so the weight's role in scaling the input value is visible in the propagation itself; the trails converge at each hidden neuron and merge into a single intermediate sum that appears inside the neuron; the activation function is applied to that sum with a brief visual squashing motion on the value (the value visibly compresses or stretches inside the neuron); the resulting post-activation value is then visible inside the hidden neuron; the flow then continues from the hidden layer to the output neuron with the same pattern - trails along edges scaled by weight magnitude, convergence inside the output neuron, activation applied, final output value visible; brief zoom into one representative hidden neuron immediately after its activation step to make the squashing motion close-up, then zoom back out to follow the propagation to the output
  - scaling pattern: as a second beat after the slate clears, the same animation replays on the larger network (5-10-10-3); one representative trail per layer flows at full speed to make the pattern clear; the remaining trails for the same layer flow in parallel at higher speed; the focus stays on the propagation pattern, not on every individual trail; the squashing-motion zoom-in happens once on a representative neuron in the first hidden layer
